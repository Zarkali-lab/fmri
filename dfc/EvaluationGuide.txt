# Evaluation of Clustering Methods

## Elbow Method:
The elbow method is used to find the optimal number of clusters by plotting the sum of squared distances (inertia) from each point to its assigned cluster center as a function of the number of clusters.
You look for the "elbow" or the point where the rate of decrease sharply slows down. 
This point is often the optimal number of clusters, as adding more clusters beyond it yields diminishing returns in reducing inertia.

## Silhouette Score:
The silhouette score ranges from -1 to 1, indicating how similar each point is to its own cluster (cohesion) compared to other clusters (separation).
- A score close to 1 indicates well-separated clusters
- 0 suggests overlapping clusters, and 
- negative values imply points are potentially in the wrong clusters.
The optimal number of clusters often maximizes the average silhouette score across all points.

## Davies-Bouldin Index:
This metric evaluates the ratio of within-cluster dispersion to between-cluster separation.
Lower values indicating better clustering.
Each cluster is evaluated on how closely its points are grouped compared to the distance to the nearest cluster, and the final score is the average across all clusters.
The ideal cluster count minimizes the Davies-Bouldin index, suggesting well-defined and separated clusters.

## Calinski-Harabasz Index:
Also known as the Variance Ratio Criterion, measures the ratio of the sum of between-cluster dispersion to within-cluster dispersion, giving an idea of cluster compactness and separation.
Higher values indicate better-defined clusters since the between-cluster distances are high and the within-cluster distances are low.
- Particularly useful for larger datasets as it doesn’t suffer from the same drawbacks as the silhouette core in high-dimensional space.
- Aim for the configuration with the highest Calinski-Harabasz index

# In summary:
1. Use the elbow for a rough estimate of cluster numbers.
2. Verify with the silhouette score for separation quality. Ideally closer to 1
3. Confirm by minimizing the Davies-Bouldin and maximising Calinski-Harabasz for well-defined clusters.
